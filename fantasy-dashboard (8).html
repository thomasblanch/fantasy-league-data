Roto Power Ranking

💡
What this tells youWho's actually the best team, independent of a lucky or unlucky schedule. Every week, each team gets graded 1st-to-10th in each of the 9 stat categories, and those grades are averaged all season. It answers "who would win if everyone played everyone, every week?" — a fairer measure than the standings alone.
How this is calculated
  • Each week, in every one of the 9 categories, teams are ranked 1st–10th and awarded points: 10 for 1st down to 1 for last (standard "roto" scoring).
  • A team's weekly total is the sum of those points across all 9 categories (max possible: 90/week).
  • The number shown is that weekly total averaged across every week played this season — it ignores who a team's actual opponent was that week.
  • Turnovers (TO) are ranked in reverse — fewer turnovers ranks higher.

Category Luck

💡
What this tells youWhether a team's record is deserved or just down to a soft schedule. It compares the categories a team actually won against their real opponent to how many categories they'd have won on average against every other team that week. A big positive number means they're winning close calls; a big negative number means they're better than their record shows.
How this is calculated
  • Actual = total categories won in a team's real matchups, summed across the season (max 9 per week).
  • Expected = each week, the team's stat line is compared against all 9 other teams individually (not just their real opponent); the average number of categories they'd beat each of those 9 teams in is added to a running season total.
  • Luck = Actual − Expected. Positive means their real opponents let them win more categories than the league average would; negative means the opposite.

This Week's Storylines

💡
What this tells youA quick, shareable recap of the week's headlines — who dominated, who had the closest call, who had a rough week, and whether anyone nearly swept every category. Built for dropping straight into the group chat.
How this is calculated
  • Manager of the Week / Rough Week: ranked by average categories beaten against all 9 other teams that week (same "expected" method as Category Luck) — not by their actual matchup result.
  • Biggest Heartbreak: the real matchup this week with the smallest gap between categories won and lost (closest to 5-4).
  • Sweep of the Week: any real matchup this week where one team won 8 or all 9 categories; shows "None" if no matchup reached that.

Season Category Records (W-L-T)

💡
What this tells youFor each of the 9 categories, how many individual category battles a team has actually won, lost, or tied against real opponents this season — like a mini win-loss record inside every stat. Handy for spotting exactly which categories are winning or losing matchups.

Category Rank Heatmap

💡
What this tells youWhere each team ranks league-wide (1 = best, 10 = worst) in each category's season average. Because it's a rank rather than a raw number, you can fairly compare a team's standing in points against their standing in free-throw percentage — something raw totals can't do. Greener/darker cells mean a stronger rank.
How this is calculated
  • W-L-T table: tallies outcomes from a team's real weekly matchups only — this is what actually happened, category by category, opponent by opponent.
  • Rank heatmap: uses a team's season-average value in each category (not matchup outcomes) to rank all 10 teams 1–10. A team can rank highly here while having a losing record in that category's W-L-T if their raw production is strong but their opponents happened to be stronger.
  • A tie in the W-L-T table only occurs when two teams post the exact same value in a category the same week — rare with real stats.

Punt Detector

💡
What this tells you"Punting" is when a team deliberately (or accidentally) gives up on a category to focus elsewhere — like ignoring blocks to load up on assists. This flags any team that's finished in the bottom 3 of a category in 60% or more of the weeks so far, which is a strong sign they've written that category off. Useful ammo for trade talks.
How this is calculated
  • Each week, the 3 teams with the worst value in a category (lowest for most stats, highest turnovers for TO) are marked "bottom-3" for that category-week.
  • A team-category pair is flagged as a punt if it was bottom-3 in 60% or more of weeks played this season. The 60% threshold is adjustable in the code.
  • This only looks at finishing position, not intent — a team could show up here from bad luck rather than a deliberate punt strategy.

Category MVP by Team

💡
What this tells youEvery roster has one player quietly carrying more weight than the rest. This shows who that is for each team, and which single category they contribute the most to — the player you'd most want to ask about in a trade.
How this is calculated
  • In the sample data, each player has a randomly generated "contribution share" per category — this is placeholder data, not real box-score attribution.
  • A player's overall value is the sum of their contribution across all 9 categories; the highest-value player per team is shown as that team's MVP.
  • To make this real, the data feed needs actual per-player per-category stat shares (e.g. each player's percentage of their team's total rebounds, assists, etc.) — the current export script does not yet calculate this.

Games Played Pace

💡
What this tells youIn counting categories (points, rebounds, assists, etc.), a team whose players simply play more games has a built-in edge — nothing to do with skill. This shows each team's total games played this season and whether last week's schedule was favorable (above league average) or thin (below it).
How this is calculated
  • Total games played is summed across every week of the season so far.
  • The "vs avg last wk" figure only compares the most recent week against that same week's 10-team league average — it does not reflect the whole season's pace.
  • Games played here reflects roster construction and streaming, not games actually started or points scored.

Best Waiver Wire Pickups

💡
What this tells youWhich free-agent adds have actually paid off since a manager picked them up — the "found gold on the waiver wire" leaderboard. Great for calling out a lucky streaming pickup, or admitting someone else out-scouted the league.
How this is calculated
  • The "value" number is whatever figure is supplied in the loaded data's transactions list — in the sample data it's a randomly generated placeholder, not a real stat.
  • For real data, this needs a defined formula (e.g. total fantasy points, or category-adjusted value, produced by that player since the add date) — the current export script stubs this at 0 and needs that logic added.
  • Only the most recent ~25 add transactions are considered, and the top 7 by value are shown.

Playoff Odds

💡
What this tells youEach team's chance of finishing in a playoff spot, estimated by simulating the rest of the season thousands of times. Every simulated week, each remaining matchup is decided the same way a real one would be — category by category — based on how each team has actually been performing.
How this is calculated
  • Each team's season-average and week-to-week variability in each category becomes a bell-curve model of their likely performance.
  • 1,500 full remaining seasons are simulated. Each simulated week, opponents are drawn at random, and a random performance is sampled per category per team; whoever's simulated value is better wins that category, and whoever wins the majority of the 9 categories wins the matchup.
  • Remaining weeks assumed = 20 − current week (the 20-week season-length assumption noted above). Top 6 simulated final records make the playoffs.
  • This ignores injuries, trades, and schedule strength for specific opponents — it's a statistical estimate, not a prediction.

Current Streaks

💡
What this tells youWho's rolling and who's slumping right now. The colored badge shows a current winning or losing streak in actual matchups; the "Hot/Cold" badge tracks whether a team has been scoring above or below the league average lately, even if their record doesn't show it yet.
How this is calculated
  • Win/loss streak: consecutive real matchups won or lost in a row, most recent week counting backward.
  • Hot/Cold streak: consecutive weeks a team's weekly roto score (see Roto Power Ranking) finished above or below that week's 10-team average, not a fixed number.
  • Both streaks reset to zero the moment the result flips, even by a single category or point.

Championship Odds Over Time

💡
What this tells youHow each team's playoff chances have risen and fallen as the season's gone on — so you can watch a team's stock climb after a big trade, or watch it crater after a rough stretch. Assumes a 20-week regular season; adjust TOTAL_SEASON_WEEKS in the code if your league runs longer or shorter.
How this is calculated
  • Each checkpoint on the chart re-runs the same simulation as the Playoff Odds tab, but using only the data available through that week (fewer trials — 300 instead of 1,500 — to keep it fast, so this trend line is noisier than the main Playoff Odds number).
  • The chart starts a few weeks into the season once there's enough data to model each team's variability.
  • A callout appears when a team's simulated odds move 15 percentage points or more between two consecutive checkpoints.

Sweeps & Near-Sweeps

💡
What this tells youAny matchup where one team won 8 or all 9 categories — a total beatdown worth remembering (or living down).

Nail-Biter Percentage Categories

💡
What this tells youField goal and free throw percentage often come down to a fraction of a percent. This surfaces matchups where one of those categories was decided by a razor-thin margin — the kind of finish that makes someone wish they'd started one more player.
How this is calculated
  • Sweeps: any real matchup decided 8-1 or 9-0. The 6 most recent are shown.
  • Nail-biters: only checks FG% and FT%, and flags a matchup where those percentages differed by less than 0.6 percentage points (0.006) between the two teams that week. The 6 most recent are shown.

Trade Grade Leaderboard

What this tells youWho has come out ahead across all the trades logged this season, added up into one running total. Positive means a manager has generally received more value than they've given up in trades; negative means the opposite.

Log a Trade

💡
What this tells youLog any trade here — just type each player's name and their real season z-score value fills in automatically. Only add a ":value" if you want to override it (e.g. a player just back from injury whose season average overstates their current form).

Trade History

How this is calculated
  • Player "value" is a z-score total: for each of the 9 categories, a player's season average is compared to the league-wide average and standard deviation across all rostered players, giving a "how many standard deviations above/below average" score. Those 9 scores are summed into one number (turnovers inverted, since fewer is better). This is the same approach sites like Basketball Monster and ESPN's Player Rater use.
  • If you type a player's name without a value, the dashboard looks up this real z-score automatically from the loaded league data. Add :value after a name to override it — useful for a player whose season average doesn't reflect their current form (recent injury, role change, etc).
  • A player not found in the league data is counted as 0 and flagged in the status message — usually means a name typo or a rookie/free agent not in the export yet.
  • A trade's grade is based on the absolute difference between the two sides' summed value: under 3 = Fair Deal, under 8 = Slight Edge, under 15 = Lopsided, 15+ = Highway Robbery. These thresholds are adjustable in the code.
  • The leaderboard sums, for each team, (value received − value given) across every logged trade — it does not account for how those players actually performed afterward.
  • Caveat: z-score value doesn't know your team's punt strategy — a player who's elite in a category you've punted is worth less to you specifically than this number suggests.
